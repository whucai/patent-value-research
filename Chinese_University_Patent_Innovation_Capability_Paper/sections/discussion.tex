
\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Beyond Patent Evaluation}

The study demonstrates that patent quantity cannot fully represent technological development. Knowledge evolution provides a process-based explanation of why universities with similar outputs may achieve different technological influence.

\subsection{Contribution to Science Mapping}

By extending knowledge mapping from publications to patents, this study expands science mapping toward technological knowledge systems. Multi-layer representations capture conceptual, relational, and diffusion dimensions simultaneously.

\subsection{Implications for Innovation Governance}

Universities should not be evaluated only through uniform output indicators. Different evolutionary trajectories require differentiated support strategies.

\subsection{Theoretical Contribution}

The framework contributes a dynamic perspective that explains technological development as a process of knowledge transformation.
