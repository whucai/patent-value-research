\begin{structuredabstract}
\medskip\textbf{Introduction.} Evaluation of university patent technological innovation capability has long relied on result-oriented indicators such as patent counts, grants, and citation frequency. These indicators reflect mainly the scale or external impact of innovative output and reveal little about the internal capabilities---knowledge sourcing, recombination, and diffusion---that operate during the innovation process. As university patent output keeps growing, identifying this capability and its formation mechanisms from the knowledge-association structures carried by patents has become a central problem in innovation evaluation. Conventional approaches under-represent the formation process, limiting understanding of how innovation advantages arise and how resources should be allocated differentially.

\medskip\textbf{Method.} This paper proposes a measurement framework for university patent technological innovation capability based on patent knowledge-association structures. Unlike evaluation built on a single output indicator or a single patent relation, patents are treated as relational objects embedded in a technical-knowledge system. Three relation layers---IPC classification, semantic similarity, and citation linkage---are fused into a multi-dimensional knowledge-association structure, and capability is characterized along five dimensions: knowledge sourcing, knowledge recombination, technological exploration, knowledge diffusion, and dynamic transformation.

\medskip\textbf{Analysis.} A multi-relation patent knowledge-association structure is constructed from invention-patent data for 124 Chinese Double-First-Class universities over 1990--2019. Capability profiles are computed per university-period, their structural features are described, and development patterns are identified through a priori criteria cross-checked with Gaussian-mixture grouping and first-order Markov transitions, controlling for patent volume, technological composition, and coverage.

\medskip\textbf{Results.} Universities with similar patent output differ significantly in their knowledge-capability structures, showing that traditional output indicators do not fully reveal university patent technological innovation capability. Three typical development patterns are identified---steady utilization, exploratory innovation, and structural recombination---revealing distinct innovation paths in knowledge accumulation, cross-domain recombination, and technological diffusion.

\medskip\textbf{Conclusion.} The contribution is to extend university patent innovation-capability measurement from static output analysis to process-based analysis grounded in knowledge-association structures, offering a new perspective on the formation mechanisms of university innovation capability and methodological support for innovation evaluation and the optimal allocation of scientific and technological resources.
