\begin{structuredabstract}
\medskip\textbf{Introduction.} Universities with equal patent output can differ in how they source, combine, and pass on technical knowledge. Patent counts do not show this difference; the relations recorded in patent documents do. This study proposes a framework for reading Chinese university patents as dated relational documents and for tracing how institutional knowledge structures change.

\medskip\textbf{Method.} Three relation layers---IPC classification, title--abstract text, and citations---are kept separate. Four process dimensions (acquisition, recombination, emergence, and reuse among observed citers) describe a university-period profile, each dated by the window in which it becomes observable. Transformation is assessed independently as sustained change in a university's technological composition, tested against a within-university null.

\medskip\textbf{Analysis.} A fixed-layout technology map with period overlays, profile distributions adjusted for volume and field, and ordered trajectories combined with the transformation test constitute the analysis. The design states which measures a university-only patent and citation corpus can support and excludes reuse depth and economy-wide diffusion from primary claims.

\medskip\textbf{Results.} The paper provides the operational definitions and reporting design; the source database has not yet been analysed, so no profile value, trajectory count, or transformation event is reported as a finding.

\medskip\textbf{Conclusion.} The framework links knowledge organization and patent mapping to longitudinal institutional analysis while keeping each inference traceable to the patent relation that supports it and to the boundaries of the available data.
\end{structuredabstract}
